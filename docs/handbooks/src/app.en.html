1. About the app

The app puts a supplier's monthly Green Leaf Account on their phone, and lets them ask the factory for an advance, a loan, manure or tea packets, change their savings or bank details, and send the office a message. Each factory has its own branded app with its own name, colours and logo.

What the app never does
  • It works out no figure of its own. Every amount, limit and balance is the factory's, sent by the server. The app only shows it.
  • It decides nothing. Every request goes to the office; the factory approves or rejects it.
  • It shows only the supplier's own records, never another supplier's.
  • It shows no figure before the auction. A month without its rate shows blanks with a note, never "Rs. 0".

2. Signing in and keeping the app safe

  • Sign in with the supplier number and a password: the bare number from "Sup. Code" on the slip (for example 5708), never an email.
  • The first password comes from the office. At the first sign-in the app asks the supplier to choose their own. Keeping the office's password is offered as a real choice, not hidden. The answer is remembered for the account, so it is asked once, not on every phone.
  • Forgot password: there is no email reset. The app shows the factory's telephone, email and hours; the office issues a new one-time password and ends the supplier's sessions on every phone.
  • Saved login is kept only in the phone's secure keychain.
  • App PIN (4 digits) and fingerprint or face unlock: offered once after the first sign-in. Fingerprint or face appears only when the phone has one set up and the factory allows it. It unlocks a saved session; it is not a password to the server.
  • Auto-lock asks for the PIN after leaving the app: immediately, after 1 minute, after 5 minutes, or never. Only when a PIN is set. Answering a call or the app switcher does not count as leaving.
  • Coming back opens where the supplier left off, behind the lock screen if a PIN is set.
  • Session ended: if the server no longer accepts the sign-in, the app tries once to renew it; if that fails it says "Your session has ended. Please sign in again."

4. The Account tab: the monthly bill

  • Shows the newest published bill. A bill the office is still checking, or one held back, is never shown. With no bill at all: "No bill is available yet."
  • Laid out like the printed slip: factory name, telephone, registration number and month at the top; rate and kilos; amounts; the nine deductions; balance; carried-forward and savings panels; legal footer.
  • Daily supply: a calendar of days 1 to 31 with the kilos supplied each day (blank for no leaf), and a small bar chart. Under it, where the month's leaf was weighed, for example "Weighed at MAKADURA (days: 16)", once the server sends the points.
  • PDF: the bill can be saved or shared as a PDF identical to the screen.
  • "Ask about this bill" opens an inquiry about that month.
  • The labels are the slip's: "Loan (instalment 3)" for the loan line (the PDF prints the slip's "Loans / Advance 3"), and "Other deductions" with the office's reason under it.
  • Loan balance is the loan still owed; Loan interest is this month's interest in rupees.
  • Correction: when the office finds a mistake on an earlier bill, the next bill carries a line "Correction (month)" with the office's reason under it: a plus when the supplier was paid too little, a minus when paid too much. The earlier bill is never changed. A loan, fertilizer or savings balance the office corrects simply shows its new figure.
  • No signal: the app shows the copy saved on the phone (kept up to seven days). If it cannot reach the factory, every screen showing saved figures or messages shows a "Saved copy" notice with the date and time the figures were saved, and a Retry. Credit limits and the tea packet allowance are never shown from a saved copy.

5. How the bill adds up

The formula
Green leaf amount = kilos × rate per kg Extra payment = kilos × extra rate per kg Gross amount = green leaf + extra + coins brought forward Balance amount = gross − total of the nine deductions Paid = balance (+ any savings withdrawal), rounded DOWN to the factory's payment unit (Rs. 1, 5 or 10) Coins carried fwd = what is left below the unit, paid next month

If the balance is below zero, nothing is paid and the shortfall is "Next month deb", which is next month's "Previous debts".

6. Where the figures come from

The supplier's app looks the same whichever way the factory works. Behind it, the bill comes one of three ways:

The factoryThe bill in the app is
Connects its own system to us (sync on)The factory system's own bill, copied every hour
Keeps records in the console and imports its printed bills (galaboda)The printed slip, stored exactly as printed
Keeps records in the console and has bills calculated thereCalculated by the console, and compared with the printed slip to the rupee before it is shown
So the app and the paper slip always agree
  • The app never shows a bill the office has not published.
  • A calculated bill that does not match the paper slip is held back; the supplier sees "No bill is available yet" for that month until it is fixed, compared again and released.
  • A published bill is never changed. A mistake is corrected on the next month's bill.
  • Everything approved in the app is also entered by the office in the factory's own system, which prints the slip and pays the money.

The full explanation of each mode, and every reason a figure can differ, is in the console guide, section 6.

7. This month so far Coming

Where the factory turns it on, a card above the bill shows the month whose bill is next:

  • Kilos so far, days with leaf, the last supply date and a strip of daily bars.
  • The advance, loan, manure and tea packets approved in the month.
  • No money: the rate comes after the auction. The card says the payment comes with the bill.
  • When the month ends the card says "Month ended, bill being prepared" and stays until that bill is published.
  • Which month: the supplier's oldest month that still has an unpublished or held bill; otherwise the month after the factory's newest published month.

8. Income history

  • "Income" is the bill's gross amount everywhere: the chart, the list and the credit limits.
  • Three views: Graph (earnings or kilos per month across a year), List (each month's kilos, amount and paid; tap to open the bill), Chart (one month's deductions as a ring).
  • A month without its rate shows its kilos and no money, with a "waiting for the auction" mark.
  • The year's income statement can be saved as a PDF in the app's language.

9. Credit screens: how they all behave

Advance, loan, manure and tea packets each have a screen in the side menu. They work the same way:

  1. The rule first, in one sentence, so the supplier knows the policy before asking.
  2. The working: months of history, rate × kilos or the average, the multiplier, any cap, then the limit. Every figure is the factory's own. If the server sent too little to explain a figure, the limit is shown without a working rather than a wrong one.
  3. Waiting and retry: while the figures load, the screen waits; if the call fails it offers a retry, never a placeholder "0".
  4. Blocked: when a request cannot be made, there is no form. A message says why (table below).
  5. Checked as typed: an amount above the limit is refused on the spot.
  6. History: past requests with status (pending, approved, rejected, cancelled) and the office's note.
  7. Cancel: a pending request can be cancelled. If the office decided first, the cancel is refused.
  8. One tap, one request: tapping twice on a bad connection still sends one request.

10. Request an advance

  • Cash against leaf already supplied this month.
  • Limit = last settled month's rate × this month's kilos so far. So it is 0 at the start of the month, and grows as leaf is weighed.
  • No history needed.
  • Deducted in full on this month's bill, even if it is more than the month earned; the rest becomes next month's debt.
  • Not offered while an earlier advance is not yet deducted, or while any loan is owed.
  • The screen's "Interest (advance and loan)" row is the newest bill's interest charge, mostly the loan's.
Example

September rate Rs. 200 a kg; 300 kg supplied so far in October. Limit: Rs. 60,000. The supplier asks for Rs. 40,000; once approved, it comes off the October bill in full.

11. Request a loan

  • Cash against a record of income. Needs 6 settled months (the factory may set another number).
  • Limit = 3 × the average gross of the last 6 settled months (default), capped where the factory sets a maximum.
  • The supplier chooses the term: 3 to 12 monthly bills (default), as a row of choices. The amount per bill is shown under the choice as the amount is typed, so the supplier sees whether it is affordable.
  • Each bill deducts the same instalment, numbered ("Loan (instalment 3)"), but never more than the factory's share of that month's gross; in a poor month the loan simply runs longer.
  • Interest is the factory's: 0% unless set. Simple interest on the loan owed at the start of the month, taken on top of the instalment or inside it.
  • While a loan is owed, neither a new loan nor an advance can be asked for. Manure is not affected.
Example
Last 6 settled months' gross average Rs. 80,000 → limit Rs. 240,000 Asks Rs. 50,000 over 10 bills → Rs. 5,000 a bill With 1% interest on top: first bill deducts 5,500, loan balance 45,000 With 1% inside the instalment: deducts 5,000, loan balance 45,500

12. Request manure

  • Fertilizer on credit. Needs 6 settled months (default) and room under the limit.
  • Limit = last settled month's rate × that month's kilos (default). What is already owed counts against it.
  • The supplier picks the type from the factory's list, the quantity in kilos (25 to 400 in steps of 25, or any other figure), the term (1 to 6 bills by default) and delivery notes.
  • No amount is typed. The factory prices it from its own list, by the bag, rounded up to whole bags.
  • A type the factory has not priced cannot be sent.
Example

Urea in 50 kg bags at Rs. 9,000. The supplier asks for 60 kg: the store issues 2 bags, Rs. 18,000, recovered over the term chosen.

13. Request tea packets

  • The factory's own tea for the household. Not credit: no history needed.
  • The cost is shown as the number is typed, at the factory's packet price, and comes off the next bill's Tea line.
  • A monthly allowance (default 10 packets); the screen shows what is left this month and hides the form when it is used up.
  • Delivery is a choice: collect at the factory, or on the vehicle that collects the leaf.
  • If the factory has set no price: "The factory has not set a price yet", and no form.
Example

400 g packets at Rs. 1,200. Three packets: Rs. 3,600 on the next bill; 7 left for the month.

14. Savings

  • The savings account: every month's savings in and withdrawals out, oldest first, with the balance after each, and a chart.
  • Savings per kg is chosen from the factory's amounts. "No savings" (0) means the supplier is not saving.
  • Changing the amount is a request to the factory: only approval changes it, one request at a time, and it starts from the next month not yet closed.
  • Withdrawals are recorded by the office and approved by the manager, in the factory's withdrawal month (April by default). They are paid on the next bill, after the balance.
  • Interest, where the factory pays it: simple, on each month's lowest balance (the balance at the start of the month, or after a withdrawal paid that month), at the factory's yearly rate ÷ 12. It is added once a year, as one line in the savings list, with the month before the withdrawal month, so it can be taken out in April. Example at 6%: Rs. 20,000 all year earns Rs. 1,200.00.
Example

Rs. 10 a kg, 509.5 kg in July: Rs. 5,095 saved. Previous 21,970 + 5,095 = 27,065 to date.

While a change waits

The app keeps showing the current payment method or bank account, with "pending approval" beside the requested one. Money keeps going to the approved account until the factory approves the change. Approved: the new value appears on the next refresh, from the next bill. Rejected: nothing changes, and the factory's note is shown. Only one pending request of each kind.

16. Inquiries

  • Each inquiry is a conversation: the supplier's message, the office's replies and follow-ups, in one thread.
  • A message is at most 2,000 characters.
  • When the office closes it, the message box is replaced by a note; a new inquiry can be started.
  • The list is newest first, with status: waiting, resolved or closed.

17. News, banners and notifications

  • News: the factory's articles, newest first, pull down to refresh. An article with only English text still shows to a Sinhala or Tamil reader.
  • Banners: a picture or panel on the way in, shown only during the factory's chosen dates. Its button opens a screen in the app, a website, a phone call or an email.
  • Notifications: Monthly account ready, Request decided, Reply to your inquiry, Factory news (and Daily leaf weight, understood by the app but not sent yet).
  • The bell list is the factory's record, not the phone's: a push that was swiped away or blocked is still in the list. Tapping one opens what it is about and marks it read.
  • Settings → Notifications: a switch for each kind. The factory never sends a kind the supplier turned off.

19. Languages, dates and money

  • Every word on every screen, the bill labels, month names and day names come from the app's language files: Sinhala, English and Tamil.
  • Money: Rs. 1,234,567.50 in English, රු. in Sinhala, ரூ. in Tamil, always grouped in thousands with two decimals.
  • Dates follow the language: Sinhala puts the year first (2026 ජූලි 10), English and Tamil the day first.
  • Kilograms: kg, කි.ග්‍රෑ., கிலோ.
  • The printed PDF bill follows the app's language too.

21. Privacy: what the app sends

  • Crash reports (what broke, on which screen) and usage counts (which screens are used) help the team fix problems. A factory can turn either off.
  • Never sent: the supplier's name, phone, ID number, bank account, or an exact amount. Amounts are sent only as a range (for example 5,000 to 25,000). The supplier is a code number, not a name.
  • Personal and bank data are handled under Sri Lanka's Personal Data Protection Act No. 9 of 2022.
